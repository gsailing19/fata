How fata treats your data

In plain language. No lies.

1. What can fata see?

Your words. That's it. When you write something, fata's AI (running in your browser) reads your text to create a "frequency fingerprint" — a mathematical summary of the emotional tone. This fingerprint is then compared against others in the matching pool. The raw text you write is encrypted before it ever leaves your browser.

2. Does fata know my email?

No. Your email is encrypted in your browser using AES-256-GCM before it enters the matching pool. The encryption key never leaves your browser. Only when a match happens do both sides receive each other's email — and even then, fata only passes it along, it never stores it in readable form.

3. Does fata sell my data?

There is nothing to sell. fata has no servers, no database, no user accounts. The matching pool is stored in GitHub Issues — encrypted embeddings that are meaningless to anyone without your browser's encryption key. fata cannot read your words. fata cannot read your email. fata cannot read the conversations you have after matching.

4. Can anyone intercept my communication?

After matching, you and the other person communicate through your own email — Gmail, QQ Mail, Outlook, whatever you use. fata is no longer involved. Your email provider's security is your security. fata only does one thing: introduces two people.

5. How can I verify this?

fata is open source. Every line of code is publicly visible at github.com/gsailing19/fata. fata is a single static HTML file — you can open it in any text editor and read exactly what it does. There are no hidden servers, no analytics, no tracking. What you see is what exists.

6. What if I want my data deleted?

Your encrypted embedding in the matching pool expires automatically after 3 days. If you want it removed sooner, open an issue on the GitHub repository with "data deletion" in the title. Since all data is encrypted end-to-end, we'll need your browser's session identifier to locate your entry.
